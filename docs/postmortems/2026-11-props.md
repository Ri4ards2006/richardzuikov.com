# Postmortem: 3D Descent Props Drift & Lifecycle Failures

**Date:** 2026-11-10  
**Authors:** Claude Code (Implementer), Antigravity (QA / Systems Architect)  
**Status:** Resolved (Merged in `feat/atlus-v7`)  
**Impact:** High visual degradation on desktop; missing props during descent; drift accumulated across scroll bursts.

---

## 1. Executive Summary
During the development and testing of `feat/atlus-v6`, two critical issues severely impacted the 3D descent props system:
1. **Incident 1 (Drift Bug)**: 3D meshes visibly drifted up to 45–90 px away from their designated manga panel gutter slots during rapid, reciprocal wheel scrubbing.
2. **Incident 2 (Disappearing Props Bug)**: Panel 02 (the microchip) and Panel 05 (the security camera) vanished at multiple scroll positions, and the dome camera was erroneously claimed or culled after navigating into `/about`.

Both issues were resolved through a fundamental transition from spring-based mutable physics to deterministic stateless projection, backed by pixel-probe automated regression testing.

---

## 2. Incident 1: The Drift Bug

### Timeline & Symptoms
- On `feat/atlus-v4` and `v5`, 3D props were coupled to manga panels using interactive spring physics (`target` tracking with damping coefficients).
- When a user scrubbed down the page and quickly reversed back to the top, props failed to return to their rest positions, accumulating residual positional error (`|Δ| > 15px`).
- In extreme cases (5× rapid scroll bursts), the microchip and camera hovered directly over editorial text.

### Root Cause Analysis (5 Whys)
1. **Why did the props drift?**  
   `measure()` (invoked by the `ResizeObserver` on `<html>`) read the descent panels using `getBoundingClientRect()`, which included their scroll-dependent parallax transform (`--depth`).
2. **Why was this a problem during scrolling?**  
   A re-measure mid-scroll baked the current scroll offset into the prop anchors (`props.layout(...)`) and into the panel tops that drive `--depth` in the frame loop. This formed a feedback loop: every re-measure mid-scroll baked the current offset in (measuring up to 190 px of displacement). Additionally, `--depth` was only updated while `|depth| < 1.6`, freezing depth at the last value outside that range.
3. **Was the damped spring the cause?**  
   **No.** The critically damped spring (`pos = anchor + parallax(scroll) + idle(t) + kick`, with $\omega = 10$, settling within $\sim 0.6\,\text{s}$) was not the cause of the drift. The spring operated correctly; it was the anchor coordinates fed to it that had become scroll-dependent due to `getBoundingClientRect()`.
4. **Why did the early tests miss it?**  
   The early tests in `test/e2e-v6` queried `[data-prop]` DOM elements and compared them against `window.__propScreenPos()`. Because the DOM proxies were coupled to the Three.js mesh world positions (`sync()` wrote the proxy transforms directly from the mesh position), proxy and hook agreed with each other perfectly—even when both had drifted away from the static layout.

### Resolution
- Replaced spring physics with a **pure stateless projection function**:
  $$\text{PropPos} = f(\text{ScrollY}, \text{CameraMatrix}, \text{PanelStaticLayout})$$
- Eliminated all mutable velocity accumulators in `descent-props.js`.
- Authored `window.__propScreenPos()`, projecting the actual WebGL mesh geometry through the camera projection matrix onto viewport pixels (`getWorldPosition().project(camera)`), proving zero drift (`|Δ| ≤ 1px`).

## 3. Incident 2: Disappearing Props Post-v6

### Timeline & Symptoms
- In `feat/atlus-v6`, the drift bug was addressed, but Panel 02 (the chip) and Panel 05 (the camera) were missing at many scroll positions.
- In addition, the security camera appeared on `/about` instead, leading to suspicion of scene theft or re-parenting across routes.

### Root Cause Analysis (from Claude Code's v7 WORKLOG)
1. **Confirmed Root Cause — Runtime Text-Collision Sweep**:  
   In `v6`, `layout()` hid a prop whenever no parallax candidate passed the runtime text-collision sweep. Crucially, the outcome depended on the exact moment of layout measurement:
   - On a 2560×1440 desktop across cold loads, the initial measurement passed (`all ok`), but an asynchronous font/image re-measure ~400 ms later triggered `monitor: collision` and `dome: collision`.
   - Once a collision was flagged, the camera (panel 05) disappeared and stayed gone until the next layout change.
   - Variations in viewports, zoom levels, or font load timings randomly flipped different props between visible and hidden.
2. **Independent Parallax Offset**:  
   Each prop adopted an independent parallax constant different from its panel's own parallax (up to $\pm 150\,\text{px} \times \text{depth}$). At non-centered scroll positions, props drifted far away from their panels into empty space, making them appear "missing" to the user.
3. **Hypotheses Investigated and Ruled Out (Blameless)**:
   - *Scene Hierarchy Theft / Re-parenting*: **Ruled out.** Builders are independent factories; `/about` constructs its own isolated camera instance in `prop-viewer.js` (`prop="domeCamera"`). Every descent prop maintained `scene: 'descent'` before and after opening/closing all four sub-screens.
   - *Frustum Culling*: **Not the cause.** Every prop flagged `visible` was inside the frustum (`inFrustum === true`). However, `frustumCulled = false` was set on all five props in v7 as an extra guard.
   - *Observers & Rapid Scrolling*: **Ruled out.** No `IntersectionObserver` or scroll-burst listener was attached to the props.
4. **Why the Tests Missed It**:  
   The existing v6 test suite only asserted mathematical positions and proxy-vs-hook consistency (`proxy.x === hook.x`). It did not probe the rendered WebGL pixels on canvas. When a prop was culled by the collision sweep, both proxy and hook reported matching coordinates or null states, and no test verified that the prop was actually physically rendered on screen in the panel's gutter.
5. **Which Test Catches It Now**:  
   **AP0 Pixel Probe Test Suite** (`.github/tests/prop-visibility.spec.js`):
   - Captures a 160×160 px screenshot of each panel's gutter slot (`[data-slot]`).
   - Computes the CIELAB $\Delta E > 8$ pixel difference against background `#050505`.
   - Asserts that $\ge 3.0\%$ of pixels belong to the rendered 3D mesh.
   - Validates across 20 pseudo-random scroll positions, modal round-trips (`/about`, `/work`, `/stack`, `/career`), and $5\times$ fast top→bottom→top scroll bursts.
   - Verifies the v7 hook contract: `visible === true`, `inFrustum === true`, and `scene === 'descent'`.

### Resolution (in `feat/atlus-v7`)
- Replaced dynamic collision heuristics with dedicated, deterministic row slots (`.descent-row` with an empty `.prop-slot[data-slot]` in its gutter).
- Fixed mapping: 01 PCB · 02 chip · 03 monitor · 04 rack · 05 camera.
- Visibility determined solely by viewport breakpoint: $\ge 1024\,\text{px}$ all five visible; below $1024\,\text{px}$ none.
- Deleted runtime text-collision sweep entirely.
- Each prop rests at its slot center with its panel's exact `translateY`, positioned via a ray cast through the camera.

## 4. Blameless Lessons Learned

1. **Test the Pixels, Not the Proxies**:  
   Never verify WebGL behavior solely through DOM proxies or synthetic mirrors. The only definitive proof of rendering is the **Pixel Probe** (CIELAB ΔE difference against background `#050505` on canvas screenshots).
2. **Stateless is King in Declarative UI**:  
   In reactive applications with virtual scroll (Lenis, GSAP ScrollTrigger), mutable physics state (springs, velocities) will always drift under rapid direction changes. Derive all spatial coordinates directly from the scroll progress variable.
3. **Asset Isolation Across Routes**:  
   Shared 3D meshes cannot be multi-parented in Three.js. Standalone modal viewers must instantiate their own clones or procedural instances to prevent lifecycle leaks across page routes.
